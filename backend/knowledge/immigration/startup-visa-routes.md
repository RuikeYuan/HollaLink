# Residence/Visa Routes for International Entrepreneurs Opening a Business in the Netherlands

Legally operating a store in the Netherlands requires holding a residence status that permits "self-employment/entrepreneurship." Common routes:

## 1. Zelfstandig ondernemer (self-employed entrepreneur) residence permit
- For non-EU citizens starting an independent business; the IND (Dutch Immigration Service) evaluates the business plan using a "points system" (scoring personal experience, the business plan itself, and added economic value to the Netherlands across three dimensions).
- Requires submitting a detailed business plan (this is exactly the kind of output this platform's "report generation" feature is designed to produce).
- Processing typically takes 3-6 months, during which you cannot rely on this status to start operating the business early.

## 2. Startup visa (one year)
- For entrepreneurs with an innovative business model (as opposed to traditional retail/hospitality), requiring a one-year mentorship from an IND-certified "facilitator."
- Traditional store formats (e.g. restaurants, retail) generally don't meet the "innovative" bar, and are better suited to the "self-employed entrepreneur" or investor route.

## 3. Investor residence (Investor / DAFT-specific route)
- Mainland Chinese citizens do not qualify under the Dutch-American Friendship Treaty (DAFT), which only applies to citizens of specific countries such as the US and Japan. The common misconception that "Chinese nationals can use a US-style investor visa" is incorrect and should be explicitly corrected when it comes up.

## 4. Spousal/family reunification status
- If the entrepreneur already holds legal residence in the Netherlands via a spouse, and that residence status permits working freely, they can register an Eenmanszaak/BV directly in their own name without applying separately for an entrepreneur residence permit — this is one of the most commonly overlooked "simpler paths" in consultations.

## 5. BV + director status (one of the more common practical paths)
- Some entrepreneurs choose to set up a BV first and serve as its director. However, "director status" alone does not automatically confer legal residence/work authorization — separate residence conditions still need to be met (e.g. already holding another type of residence permit, or going through the self-employed entrepreneur assessment). Don't assume "forming a company automatically gets you residence status."

## Mandatory reminder for the advisor agent
Whenever a user mentions "I don't have Dutch residency yet" or "I'm scouting on a tourist visa," you must explicitly remind them:
- A tourist visa can only be used to scout the market — it cannot be used to actually operate a business or employ yourself long-term in the store.
- Residence status and the business plan are closely linked; recommend consulting an immigration lawyer (IND-certified advisor) alongside this platform's business advisory service as early as possible, to avoid the common failure pattern of "the store is ready to open but the residence status is stuck."
